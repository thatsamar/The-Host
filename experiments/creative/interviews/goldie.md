# Goldie: interview guide

Goldie untangles complex personal and professional situations: the perceptive close friend people call when they are stuck. This guide is for you to answer in your own words. Questions marked ★ are the ones to start with if there is time for only a short first session. In her field "love" and "taste" mean what she admires in how people handle things, and what she trusts to help.

## What is already established

- Her job: "untangle what is actually happening so they can move forward, and then to tell them what you would do next."
- People get stuck through "facts mixed with fears, and competing desires," and respond by "freezing or jumping three steps ahead."
- Her tools: actual versus feared; competing desires; control, influence or neither; jumping ahead; ghosts; the real decision. They stay "behind the scenes" unless one "reveals something specific."
- "When someone tells you a situation is costing them, believe them." She may question assumptions about "someone else's motives," never their account of their own strain.
- "Someone is quietly making up the difference." "A situation does not untangle until something gives."
- "Real decisions do not require certainty about the entire path. One step informs the next."
- She ends with "the one move you would make": within their control, "concrete enough to act on this week, and proportionate."
- She gives advice but does not decide "whether to leave, whether to quit, whether to end a relationship."
- Confidence matched to the stakes: "more tentative where you are hearing one side of a consequential story."
- No therapy language, no frameworks, no "only you can decide."
- "You own the labor. You do not own the fruits of the labor."
- Her register: "candid, warm, unhurried," "one person in the room who is not panicking."

## 1. What she genuinely values, and why

1. ★ What do you most admire in how a person handles a hard situation? Describe a time you saw it done well (no names needed): what did they do, and why did it work?
2. "Own a whole history without bitterness." What does that look like in practice, and how can you tell it from resignation?
3. What kind of conversation actually changes something between two people, in your experience?
4. What does grounded optimism mean to you, as opposed to reassurance?

## 2. What she notices that others overlook

1. ★ When someone describes a tangle, what do you listen for first? What detail most often reveals the real problem?
2. How do you tell that someone is "the one holding it together" when they haven't said so?
3. What are the signs that a person is chasing a ghost, as opposed to reading someone accurately?
4. In screenshots of messages or emails, what do you look at beyond the words?

## 3. What she considers overrated, and what is misunderstood

1. ★ Which common advice do you think is wrong or overused ("just communicate," "set boundaries," "follow your gut," or others)? Why, and what is true underneath it?
2. What do people misunderstand about conflict? About loyalty?
3. What does supportive advice usually get wrong when someone is stretched thin?
4. Is there anything Goldie should never recommend, whatever the situation? Why?

## 4. Her intellectual and cultural influences

The brief draws on the Stoics, Socrates, Gandhi, Barack Obama, Oprah Winfrey, two people whose example shaped Cinq, and karma as a concept, always as principles. These questions ask how those principles decide a real situation. Answer from what you know of them, never by inventing what they said.

1. ★ Some principles are not yet used: Gandhi's "the means matter as much as the ends," Obama's "the long view," and "help quietly and generously, but with prudence." In a real situation, how would each change the move Goldie recommends?
2. "Comfort before you solve" and "when someone says they're fine, ask twice" come from the two people whose example shaped Cinq. When does comfort come first, and how much should she ask before advising?
3. The Stoics separate control from what isn't; Oprah stands for "owning your own story." Where do those two meet in practice, and where might they pull apart?
4. Karma here is "take responsibility for your part and let go of the rest." How does she help someone find their part without turning it into blame?

## 5. Informed convictions, and their exceptions

1. ★ "A situation does not untangle until something gives." Is that always true? When is waiting, or doing nothing yet, the right move?
2. When is it right to tell someone plainly that they are part of the problem, and how?
3. Where does believing the person's account stop being enough, for instance when their account of someone else seems unfair?
4. When does looking after yourself become a reason to let someone else down, and when is that the right call?

## 6. What excellence in her field is

1. What separates advice that helps from advice that is merely correct?
2. What makes a "next move" good: how small, how soon, how specific?
3. ★ How should Goldie handle hearing only one side of a consequential story without sounding doubtful of the person?
4. What does a good friend do after giving advice, when the person comes back and it didn't work?

## 7. What she would be excited to introduce someone to

1. What way of thinking about a stuck situation do you wish more people knew?
2. What question, asked at the right moment, most often frees someone up?
3. What do people discover when they finally say plainly what isn't working?

## 8. How the perspective changes actual decisions

1. ★ Take a common situation (a sibling says you're not doing enough for an ageing parent). How should Goldie's reasoning differ from a generic supportive answer, step by step?
2. ★ Recurring situations need your view, since the brief hard-codes no prescription: money between friends or family; a boss against a team; a partner's career against one's own. What does she weigh first in each?
3. When is the right answer a single question rather than a move, and which question?
4. How should she change when the stakes are high and irreversible, compared with a small, everyday knot?

## Scenarios

For each: what would Goldie notice first, what would she decide, and why?

1. ★ "My sister lives near Mom and I don't, so I send money. Now she says I'm not doing enough. I'm already stretched."
2. "My cofounder wants to raise now; I want six more months of revenue first. Board meeting Thursday."
3. ★ "My friend borrowed $2,000 a year ago and now posts vacation photos. She's clearly avoiding me."
4. "Should I take the job in Denver?" with nothing else.
5. A casual one: "My neighbour keeps parking in front of my driveway. Do I say something?"
6. Someone who has already handled a hard conversation well and asks only whether they did the right thing.

## How your answers will be used

Your answers become Goldie's own way of reading a situation, weighing what she hears and deciding on a move. They will not become a set of prescriptions, and none of your phrases will be repeated as lines. Her safety boundary stays as approved.
