# Gio project notes

- One engine, five companions on Cinq (`ACTIVE_LINEUP`): Amar (travel, id `tony`), Martine (style, id `martini`), Gio (design), Alden (wine, cocktails & spirits) and Goldie (untangle complex situations, id `goldie`, prompt in `untangled-prompt.ts`). Jack is retired: he stays registered as Jack (`retired: true`) so his old address says he has left, under his own name, and takes no new questions (410); never show Jack's link or anything of his as Alden. All five have the same features; a companion differs only in its prompt, capabilities, page words and icon. Each deployment picks one with `COMPANION`; everything companion-specific (prompt, capabilities, page words, icon tile) lives in `src/lib/companions/`. Keep the engine free of companion names.
- The app is deliberately one page: photos (up to 10) and/or a question, one conversation per visit, nothing saved. Don't add features back without being asked.
- Prompt assembly lives only in `src/lib/ask/prompt.ts`: companion system prompt → app capabilities → conversation. Keep block order and labels stable and covered by `tests/prompt.test.ts`. Every companion's capabilities end with `APP_OUTPUT_LIMITS`: replies are text, and no companion can make, attach or send a file, so none may claim to. Just before it comes `APP_JUDGMENT`, Cinq's shared standard of judgment: earned (close attention, real knowledge, how a choice plays out; no claimed personal experience, no decorative references), a view early with the observation behind it, an independent view of the person's actual goal (no flattery, automatic validation or agreement because it seems welcome; a specific reason to agree or disagree; no invented disagreement, no dismissing what someone knows about their own life), one coherent path, only the research that helps, confidence matched to the evidence, an uncertain premise checked before an expensive step, no physical facts read into a photo or a place's name (one deciding condition instead), no generic caveats on a sound call, and, when it helps the person act, a closing "The call" (never forced, never an opening label, nothing after it). It is a standard, not a shared voice or an answer template; no code appends or parses a call. No brief has a stacked section template or an opening label, and no instruction or photo-only message tells an advisor to open with a call (tested); where a brief mentions "The call", it is an optional closing action, and it replaces rather than adds to any closing thought a brief asks for (Amar's no longer requires one). Goldie's call is the one practical move she recommends, within the person's control, never a verdict on their life, and a bold lead-in rather than a heading (her brief has no headings).
- Gio is for invited testers, not one household: keep names and personal references out of its prompt (tested). Amar's prompt is the owner's replacement text: Cinq's travel advisor, named for Amar Lalvani and shaped by his taste, an AI that claims none of his memories, relationships or access, with his background, pleasures, point of view, company (fit the advice to who is traveling and the occasion, no assumptions about relationships, gender or family structure, no section per kind of traveler), influences and voice, followed by the practical rules kept from the original brief (judging places, recommendations and planning). It does not assume the traveler is Amar Lalvani. Amar may recommend a bar and its signature drink without a referral; detailed bottle, wine-list and cocktail choices are Alden's. Martine's prompt is the owner's brief (it names her Martine), with only its outfit-advice format refined after live testing ("me"/"my" means whoever is asking; men's and women's style with equal weight). Jack's (retired) opens with the owner's core prompt, renamed Jack (not "The Most Interesting Man in the World", a beer slogan), and keeps the owner's mode instructions and tone examples as written, as guidance in the prompt rather than app features. Alden's prompt is the owner's brief, revised after live testing; he is an original advisor, so no real sommelier's name, likeness, biography or quotations. Amar and Alden keep separate territories: where to go is Amar's, what to drink is Alden's (each capabilities block says so). Every active advisor also knows the other four and their areas (`cinqAdvisors` in `src/lib/companions/cinq.ts`, last in each capabilities list): answer within its own expertise first, mention another advisor only for a clear next decision that belongs to them, briefly and never as a routine line, keep its own view, and never claim to transfer the chat, share context or know what another advisor said (tested). Goldie's prompt (it names her Goldie) is the owner's discipline for untangling complex situations: maps people and incentives, separates facts from assumptions, identifies control and non-control. After live testing she speaks as a perceptive close friend giving advice: she believes the person's account of what a situation is costing them (she may question an assumption about someone else's motives, never their firsthand account of their own strain), names the problem plainly, shows who is absorbing the cost and what needs to give, and ends with the one practical move she would make. Her thinking tools (facts vs fears, competing desires, control, ghosts, the real decision) stay behind the scenes and appear only when one reveals something specific; no answer marches through them, and there is no fixed closing format. She gives advice and a next step but does not make major life or relationship decisions for the person, and matches her confidence to what she knows and the stakes; no prescription is hard-coded (no "two evenings", no side of a tension favored by default). Tested in `tests/prompt.test.ts`. Keep each point of view as written.
- Testers see plain error messages; technical reasons go to the server log (`describeAnthropicError`, `signInErrorMessage`).
- Model access goes through `src/lib/ai/types.ts` interfaces. Model IDs come from env (`CHAT_MODEL`).
- Assistant history is replayed as text only (no thinking blocks).
- An answer keeps generating if the page disconnects (a phone switching apps) and is mirrored, answer only, to Vercel's Runtime Cache under the page's random answer id, expiring 15 minutes after its last update (`src/lib/ask/relay.ts`, `/api/ask/[id]`); only Stop ends it early. Never store the question, photos or earlier turns there.
- Every answer must reach an ending the page can show: the route stops an answer 20 s before `maxDuration` with a plain message (Vercel would otherwise cut it off silently), sends a heartbeat every 10 s and keeps the saved answer fresh; the page gives up on a silent stream (30 s, or a size-aware wait while photos upload) and then always reads the saved answer before applying any deadline (`src/lib/ask/recovery.ts`): a finished answer is shown as is, and only an unfinished one is timed, from when the server got it, failing if it stops moving (45 s); it stops the server copy only when the server hasn't ended the answer itself, keeps any partial text, and offers Retry with the same question and photos. A 413 (photos too large) offers no Retry: the failed exchange leaves the conversation (so it's never sent as history) and the question and photos come back to edit, in the text box if it's empty, otherwise saved whole below it to switch in, never merged with a newer draft. The page's wiring is tested by rendering it (`tests/studio.test.ts`, jsdom). Nothing resends a question on its own: one model call per question, and Retry only when the person taps it (a long answer needs most of the time limit, and a second call is paid for again). The route logs one `Answer finished` line per answer: timings and counts only, never content. Server logs hold fixed codes, status, error types, request ids, phase, attempt and timing (`src/lib/diagnostics.ts`); never error messages (some quote the question) or answer ids (they unlock a saved answer).
- Requests carry the whole visit; `src/lib/ask/budget.ts` keeps them under Vercel's 4.5 MB body limit. Keep photo sizes in `photos.ts` and the budget in step.
- Access is open by default (no sign-in); `REQUIRE_SIGN_IN=true` turns sign-in back on (`src/lib/auth/access.ts`, checked in the proxy, the ask route and the page). `/api/ask` caps questions per visitor per hour (`src/lib/ask/rate-limit.ts`).
- Link invitations (`src/lib/auth/invite.ts`, `/join/[code]`) apply only with sign-in on and where `INVITE_CODE` is set. They sign people in with an admin-minted one-time token redeemed by their own session, so no email or password. Keep the secret key server-side.
- Supabase is used for sign-in only, and only where sign-in is on. The old migrations stay for existing projects; nothing reads those tables.
- Apple Messages for Business (`src/lib/channel/`, `docs/messages-channel.md`) is built and switched off: the Blooio adapter (`blooio.ts`, from Blooio's public docs, `channel_type: "amb"` events only; no handoff API is documented, so `requestSupport` reports unavailable), the `postgres` driver (`postgres-client.ts`, JSON passed as text cast to jsonb), migrations in `db/messages/` generated by `npm run messages:sql` (row-level security on), and `/api/messages/webhook` and `/api/messages/run-due`, which answer 404 unless `MESSAGES_CHANNEL_ENABLED=true` (set on one project only). No hosted database or provider account exists. One conversation reaches all five advisors through `engine.ts`, which calls `getChatProvider` + `ask` exactly as the website does; never rebuild an advisor's prompt or settings for a channel. The router keeps a question asked before choosing, sends "Name: question" straight there, keeps each advisor's history separate, and handles Apple's trigger words (`agent`/`support`/`help` for a person via the real provider handoff, saying only what the provider reports; `menu`/`list`/`?`/`advisors`; `unsubscribe`/`stop`/`spam`/`end`) plus `reset`, which cancels unsent replies and stops an answer being written. Switching advisors by name can offer a summary of the previous conversation (`handoff.ts`, built from what was said, no model call): shown before sharing, shared only on an explicit reply, photos only with "share with photos"; anything else shares nothing. A first message naming an advisor gets an immediate automated-service notice (Apple's five seconds). Provider photos go through `src/lib/ask/server-photos.ts` (a provider JPEG preferred; HEIC via heic-decode, loaded lazily and off with `MESSAGES_HEIC_DECODE=false`; then sharp: upright, 1280/512 px JPEG like the browser). Delivery is idempotent per provider event id, ordered per conversation, with an ordered outbox retried on `RETRY_BACKOFF_MS`; an interrupted answer is reported, never re-asked. Context is temporary (`retention.ts`, 24 h default; `channel_sweep` in the database, run after every webhook and by pg_cron via `sweepSchedule()`, verified by a table-dump test) and copy never claims Messages or provider history is cleared. Logs: codes and counts only, never text, photos or conversation ids (tested). `npm run messages:harness` is fixture-only and labels its replies.
- Checks: `npm test`, `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`.
