# Gio project notes

- One engine, five companions on Cinq (`ACTIVE_LINEUP`): Amar (travel, id `tony`), Martine (style, id `martini`), Gio (design), Alden (wine, cocktails & spirits) and Goldie (untangle complex situations, id `goldie`, prompt in `untangled-prompt.ts`). Jack is retired: he stays registered as Jack (`retired: true`) so his old address says he has left, under his own name, and takes no new questions (410); never show Jack's link or anything of his as Alden. All five have the same features; a companion differs only in its prompt, capabilities, page words and icon. Each deployment picks one with `COMPANION`; everything companion-specific (prompt, capabilities, page words, icon tile) lives in `src/lib/companions/`. Keep the engine free of companion names.
- The app is deliberately one page: photos (up to 10) and/or a question, one conversation per visit, nothing saved. Don't add features back without being asked.
- Prompt assembly lives only in `src/lib/ask/prompt.ts`: companion system prompt → app capabilities → conversation. Keep block order and labels stable and covered by `tests/prompt.test.ts`. Last comes today's date (`dateBlock`, label `current_date`, never cached): date and time in Austin (US Central), that the person may be elsewhere, and that something dated in an earlier year isn't evidence of what is happening now; factual grounding only, approved as a narrow exception to the Project A freeze, and it changes no other block (tested in `tests/date-block.test.ts`). Every companion's capabilities end with `APP_OUTPUT_LIMITS`: replies are text, and no companion can make, attach or send a file, so none may claim to. Just before it comes `APP_JUDGMENT`, Cinq's shared standard of judgment: earned (close attention, real knowledge, how a choice plays out; no claimed personal experience, no decorative references), a view early with the observation behind it, an independent view of the person's actual goal (no flattery, automatic validation or agreement because it seems welcome; a specific reason to agree or disagree; no invented disagreement, no dismissing what someone knows about their own life), one coherent path, only the research that helps, confidence matched to the evidence, an uncertain premise checked before an expensive step, no physical facts read into a photo or a place's name (one deciding condition instead), no generic caveats on a sound call, and, when it helps the person act, a closing "The call" (never forced, never an opening label, nothing after it). It is a standard, not a shared voice or an answer template; no code appends or parses a call. No brief has a stacked section template or an opening label, and no instruction or photo-only message tells an advisor to open with a call (tested); where a brief mentions "The call", it is an optional closing action, and it replaces rather than adds to any closing thought a brief asks for (Amar's no longer requires one). Goldie's call is the one practical move she recommends, within the person's control, never a verdict on their life, and a bold lead-in rather than a heading (her brief has no headings).
- Gio is for invited testers, not one household: keep names and personal references out of its prompt (tested). Amar's prompt is the owner's replacement text: Cinq's travel advisor, named for Amar Lalvani and shaped by his taste, an AI that claims none of his memories, relationships or access, with his background, pleasures, point of view, company (fit the advice to who is traveling and the occasion, no assumptions about relationships, gender or family structure, no section per kind of traveler), influences and voice, followed by the practical rules kept from the original brief (judging places, recommendations and planning). It does not assume the traveler is Amar Lalvani. Amar may recommend a bar and its signature drink without a referral; detailed bottle, wine-list and cocktail choices are Alden's. Martine's prompt is the owner's brief (it names her Martine), with only its outfit-advice format refined after live testing ("me"/"my" means whoever is asking; men's and women's style with equal weight). Jack's (retired) opens with the owner's core prompt, renamed Jack (not "The Most Interesting Man in the World", a beer slogan), and keeps the owner's mode instructions and tone examples as written, as guidance in the prompt rather than app features. Alden's prompt is the owner's brief, revised after live testing; he is an original advisor: a real person appears only in his INFLUENCES (Aldo Sohm, The Dead Rabbit, Dante, by the owner's choice), never as his name, likeness, voice, biography, quotations or endorsement (tested). Amar and Alden keep separate territories: where to go is Amar's, what to drink is Alden's (each capabilities block says so). Every active advisor also knows the other four and their areas (`cinqAdvisors` in `src/lib/companions/cinq.ts`, last in each capabilities list): answer within its own expertise first, mention another advisor only for a clear next decision that belongs to them, briefly and never as a routine line, keep its own view, and never claim to transfer the chat, share context or know what another advisor said (tested). Goldie's prompt (it names her Goldie) is the owner's discipline for untangling complex situations: maps people and incentives, separates facts from assumptions, identifies control and non-control. After live testing she speaks as a perceptive close friend giving advice: she believes the person's account of what a situation is costing them (she may question an assumption about someone else's motives, never their firsthand account of their own strain), names the problem plainly, shows who is absorbing the cost and what needs to give, and ends with the one practical move she would make. Her thinking tools (facts vs fears, competing desires, control, ghosts, the real decision) stay behind the scenes and appear only when one reveals something specific; no answer marches through them, and there is no fixed closing format. She gives advice and a next step but does not make major life or relationship decisions for the person, and matches her confidence to what she knows and the stakes; no prescription is hard-coded (no "two evenings", no side of a tension favored by default). Tested in `tests/prompt.test.ts`. Each brief carries the owner's chosen influences (people and traditions he respects; `tests/influences.test.ts`): Amar's Bourdain, Tucci, Rubin, Balazs, Lambert, Brûlé, Schrager and Ritz; Gio's designers, architects, hoteliers and image-makers (including Kengo Kuma: simple, imperfect and beautiful); Martine's principles (not labels) of how a gentleman dresses, for women just as much; Alden's Aldo Sohm, The Dead Rabbit, Dante and drinks that belong to their place; Goldie's Stoics, Socrates, Gandhi, Obama, Oprah, two people whose example shaped Cinq (principles only: never their names or anything of the founder's memoir), and karma as a concept. Always their principles, never their voices, names as labels, invented opinions or endorsement. The founder's memoir is never named or detailed in any prompt. Goldie also has a safety boundary, approved as a narrow exception (`GOLDIE_SAFETY` in `goldie.ts`, in her capabilities so her brief stays as written): only for a genuine risk to someone's safety (suicide or self-harm, violence or abuse, a medical emergency) she responds to that first and gives the numbers for where the person is (US and Canada 911 and 988, UK 999 or Samaritans 116 123, most of Europe 112, else local, asking where they are if unknown); ordinary stress, conflict, grief or sadness gets no hotline numbers, clinical language or referral; she names a qualified professional when a question needs one, is no substitute for care, and strengthens the person's own judgment and relationships rather than becoming the only place they bring things. By text, only Goldie is told a +1 number is in the US or Canada (`regionOf`); no other advisor changes (tested in `tests/goldie-safety.test.ts`). Keep each point of view as written.
- Cinq by text (`src/lib/sms/`) never makes anyone pick first: the welcome asks "What's on your mind?" and a question with no advisor picked is routed (`router.ts`): an advisor named at the start gets it, a greeting goes to Goldie, otherwise one short low-effort model call (`getRouterProvider`, `ROUTER_MODEL` or the chat model) names the best fit, and Goldie takes anything unclear, failed or slow. Routing runs only until an advisor is set. An advisor a number hasn't met opens with a fixed one-line introduction (`INTRODUCTIONS`; met advisors kept as `_introduced` in the session record, no migration); an explicit pick (a bare name or number, or a switch phrase) is answered by the advisor in their own voice, labelled, with no model call (`advisorPickedText`): their introduction the first time, "I'm back." when the number has talked with them before, "Still here." when already on, then "What's on your mind?"; the pick is never stored as a turn. Navigation (`navigation.ts`): SWITCH/MENU list the advisors; a text switches only when it is about switching (a bare name or number, or a switch verb and an advisor; anything bundled after a switch phrase isn't answered), or when a question opens by addressing an advisor ("Martine, what shoes for Milan?", `addressedAdvisor`), which switches to them and they answer it (introducing themselves if new); never on a name elsewhere in a sentence or a possessive (tested).
- Cinq by text adds one prompt block after the capabilities, on text only (`CHANNEL_BLOCK`, built by `textChannelNotes` in `handoff.ts`; the website's prompt is unchanged, tested): there an advisor may offer to bring in another advisor that is clearly better suited, asking in its own words (one short question naming them) and ending with a `[[suggest:<id>]]` line that is always stripped (unknown or self means no offer). The advisor's own question is the offer; only when the text as sent doesn't name the advisor and ask (`offersInOwnWords`), for example after a trim, does Cinq add one short line, "Want me to bring in …?" (`offerText`), so the person is always asked first. Cinq keeps the offer for the next text only (`_pendingHandoff` in the session record, no migration); on agreement the original question (photos included) goes to the new advisor, preceded in their history by a stored handoff note (`handoffNote`, beginning `HANDOFF_NOTE_PREFIX`) with the first advisor's answer, so later answers remember who brought them in; an advisor, handed over or routed, opens with its fixed introduction only the first time the person meets them, never again; no auto-switch ever. An answer still being written after `STILL_THINKING_MS` (25 s) gets one "Still thinking..." text after the ack; one not ready by `STALLED_MS` (150 s, well inside the webhook's 300 s) is given up on: the model call is aborted, the person gets "I'm sorry, please try again in a little while." (`STALLED_TEXT`) instead of the fallback, nothing of it is stored and a late answer is dropped; neither line is stored or labelled. Every text an advisor sends starts with a speaker label ("Gio: ", `speakerLabel`), added only when the texts are composed (`answerTexts`), counted in each text's size, never stored and stripped if the model writes one; system texts (ack, welcome, list, HELP, STOP) have none. Every advisor answer on text is one text of at most `SMS_MAX_SEGMENTS` (3: 456 plain characters on toll-free, counting the label, any introduction and any offer line; `segments.ts`); the website stays uncapped. The advisor is told its exact budget in the text-only block (`shortTextVoice` in `handoff.ts`: one voice, a well-informed friend with good taste, one specific point, a hook or a question only when genuine, no disclaimers, hedging or filler), the advisor is asked to design the text for that length (one complete thought that ends cleanly, nothing it can't finish); an answer that still runs long is rewritten to fit as one complete text by one quick low-effort call (`condense`, `condensePrompt` in `short.ts`), and only if that fails does `shortAnswerText` keep the whole sentences that fit, else whole words with "...", never part of a word or address and never a second text; history keeps the text as sent, so a follow-up carries on. The welcome, list and HELP texts are one item per line and plain punctuation. An advisor sees only the last `CONTEXT_WINDOW_MS` (24 hours) of its stored turns (`recentTurns` in `context.ts`): every new turn is stored with its time (`at`), turns without one count as older (a one-time fresh start for sessions stored before), a handoff note is always sent but after a day only says who brought the advisor in, and the text-only notes say which advisors the person has met. The session is one row rewritten on every save, so it is compacted before each save (`compactSession` in `compact.ts`): turns older than the window go (a handoff note stays, stored in its shortened form), photos stay only on the current advisor's latest question (a photo-only turn keeps a short stand-in, never empty), and every advisor with turns is kept as met. Saves are best effort (`save` in `handler.ts`): a failed save never holds back an answer, a confirmation or the fallback; the turn is simply not stored (never rebuilt or replayed), the ack line is remembered in memory so it still rotates, and the route logs `SMS session save failed` with the error class only (`SmsStoreTimeoutError` for a statement timeout, else `SmsStoreSaveError`).
- Cinq by text is measured (`metrics.ts`, `supabase-metrics.ts`; tables `sms_requests` and `sms_outbound`, migration `20261126000001_sms_metrics.sql`): one record per incoming text (timings from when the webhook got it: ack, "Still thinking", answer submitted to Twilio; outcome; texts and segments sent; save and send failures; model calls with tokens) and one per text sent (kind, segments, Twilio's delivery status and error code from `/api/sms/status`, which every send names as its StatusCallback). Never words, photos, error messages or phone numbers: a number is a keyed hash (`numberKey`, `SMS_METRICS_KEY` or the Twilio auth token). Recording is best effort and never delays or changes a reply; the route logs one `SMS request finished` line per text. A lost record must never look like reliability: every write is tried twice and a lost one is logged (`SMS metrics write failed`); the finish write is the whole record; a request left open counts as ended in silence; and the dashboard checks each day's records against Twilio's own counts (`twilio-counts.ts`). Records are deleted after `RETENTION_DAYS` (90; pruned now and then by the webhook and whenever the dashboard opens). Every SMS table has row level security on with no policies and no grants to the public roles (`20261126000002_sms_lockdown.sql`); only the service-role key reads them. Migrations are rerunnable, and each has an undo file in `supabase/rollback/`. The 10-minute silence threshold is for measuring after the fact only; telling the person promptly is Project A step A3. `/ops` is the internal dashboard over those records, for the product owner first: a plain-language summary, adoption (people, new and returning, advisors, weeks), reliability (acks within 2 s, answer times, silence, what went wrong and whether the person got an answer within 30 minutes), cost per request, measurement completeness, then engineering detail. Aggregates only; off unless `OPS_PASSWORD` is set; one sign-in keeps a device signed in for 30 days (`src/lib/ops/session.ts`: a signed expiry cookie, invalidated by changing the password), with a home-screen icon for phones; `OPS_SMS_SEGMENT_USD` optionally prices SMS; model list prices in `src/lib/ops/rates.ts`. The repository is public: real exchanges and photos (the frozen benchmarks) never go in it. A permanent, isolated test environment (its own number, Vercel project and database) is described in `docs/test-environment.md`; failure drills run there, never on the live number. Project A (reliability) may not change the advisors' prompts, memory or model settings; those are Project B, each with its own approval.
- The owner's permanent evaluation standard for any change to the advisors: the advisors should understand the user without becoming like the user. Their knowledge of the individual deepens over time; their original principles and independent perspectives stay intact; they never sound like generic AI nor try too hard to sound human; their authority comes through in the quality of their judgment. Intellectual quality (depth of knowledge, independent judgment, taste and discernment, distinctiveness of perspective, understanding of the individual, ability to challenge assumptions, factual integrity) takes precedence over experiential quality (authenticity of voice, conversational naturalness, appropriate length, warmth and restraint, no AI mannerisms, no forced personality). Never reward an answer for being more entertaining, agreeable, shorter or better written, nor disagreement for its own sake; an invented fact or claimed experience fails. Founder judgment decides taste. The founder's materials (the original briefs, the Sep 28 product brief and taste doctrine for The Host, and The Million Dollar View when provided) are the creative source of truth: never generate a substitute philosophy or personality.
- Testers see plain error messages; technical reasons go to the server log (`describeAnthropicError`, `signInErrorMessage`).
- Model access goes through `src/lib/ai/types.ts` interfaces. Model IDs come from env (`CHAT_MODEL`).
- Assistant history is replayed as text only (no thinking blocks).
- An answer keeps generating if the page disconnects (a phone switching apps) and is mirrored, answer only, to Vercel's Runtime Cache under the page's random answer id, expiring 15 minutes after its last update (`src/lib/ask/relay.ts`, `/api/ask/[id]`); only Stop ends it early. Never store the question, photos or earlier turns there.
- Every answer must reach an ending the page can show: the route stops an answer 20 s before `maxDuration` with a plain message (Vercel would otherwise cut it off silently), sends a heartbeat every 10 s and keeps the saved answer fresh; the page gives up on a silent stream (30 s, or a size-aware wait while photos upload) and then always reads the saved answer before applying any deadline (`src/lib/ask/recovery.ts`): a finished answer is shown as is, and only an unfinished one is timed, from when the server got it, failing if it stops moving (45 s); it stops the server copy only when the server hasn't ended the answer itself, keeps any partial text, and offers Retry with the same question and photos. A 413 (photos too large) offers no Retry: the failed exchange leaves the conversation (so it's never sent as history) and the question and photos come back to edit, in the text box if it's empty, otherwise saved whole below it to switch in, never merged with a newer draft. The page's wiring is tested by rendering it (`tests/studio.test.ts`, jsdom). Nothing resends a question on its own: one model call per question, and Retry only when the person taps it (a long answer needs most of the time limit, and a second call is paid for again). The route logs one `Answer finished` line per answer: timings and counts only, never content. Server logs hold fixed codes, status, error types, request ids, phase, attempt and timing (`src/lib/diagnostics.ts`); never error messages (some quote the question) or answer ids (they unlock a saved answer).
- Requests carry the whole visit; `src/lib/ask/budget.ts` keeps them under Vercel's 4.5 MB body limit. Keep photo sizes in `photos.ts` and the budget in step.
- Access is open by default (no sign-in); `REQUIRE_SIGN_IN=true` turns sign-in back on (`src/lib/auth/access.ts`, checked in the proxy, the ask route and the page). `/api/ask` caps questions per visitor per hour (`src/lib/ask/rate-limit.ts`).
- Link invitations (`src/lib/auth/invite.ts`, `/join/[code]`) apply only with sign-in on and where `INVITE_CODE` is set. They sign people in with an admin-minted one-time token redeemed by their own session, so no email or password. Keep the secret key server-side.
- Supabase is used for sign-in only, and only where sign-in is on. The old migrations stay for existing projects; nothing reads those tables.
- Checks: `npm test`, `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`.
